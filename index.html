You are standing near one of Moraine State Park's official Eastern Bluebird Nest Boxes. Since the mid-20th century, community bluebird trails have helped restore bluebird populations across North America.

🌿 How the Trail Works:

Trained park volunteers monitor each box weekly from April through August to record egg counts, track chick growth, and safeguard nests against non-native competitors and predators.

⚠️ Please DO NOT open, tap, or touch this nest box. Disturbing the box can cause nesting parents to abandon eggs or baby birds to fledge prematurely.

🐦 Moraine State Park Bird & Nest Guide
Eastern Bluebird (Target Native)
✓ Target Native Species
♂ Male (Vivid Blue / Rust)
♀ Female (Muted Blue-Grey)

Identification: Males are vibrant royal blue with brick-red chest. Females are soft grey-blue with peach/orange wash on chest and blue highlights on wings/tail.

Nest: Neat, tidy cup made of fine dry grasses, pine needles, or weed stems.

Eggs: 4–6 clear sky-blue (occasionally pure white) unspotted eggs.

Incubation / Fledge: 12–14 days incubation; 16–21 days to fledge.

⚠️ Do NOT open the box after Day 13 of chick life to prevent premature fledging.

Tree Swallow (Native Competitor)
✓ Native Species
♂ Male (Metallic Blue-Green)
♀ Female (Brownish-Green)

Identification: Males have iridescent blue-green back and pure white belly. Females are duller with greenish-brown or brown upperparts.

Nest: Grass cup copiously lined with soft white or grey feathers curved over the eggs.

Eggs: 4–7 pure white, delicate unspotted eggs.

Note: Voracious insect eaters! If paired with a bluebird box 15–20 ft away, both species will nest peacefully side-by-side.

House Wren (Native Competitor)
✓ Native Protected
♂ / ♀ Adult (Sexes Alike)

Identification: Small, energetic plain brown bird with barred wings and tail, pale eyebrow, and cocked tail.

Nest: Fills entire box almost to the hole with small coarse sticks/twigs, lined with feathers or hair.

Eggs: 5–8 tiny pinkish-white eggs heavily speckled with cinnamon-brown dots.

Note: Wrens are legally protected under the Migratory Bird Treaty Act. Once they lay an egg, do NOT remove.

Black-capped Chickadee
✓ Native Species
♂ / ♀ Adult (Sexes Alike)

Identification: Distinct black cap and black bib, crisp white cheeks, grey back, and buff-tan flanks.

Nest: Thick soft base of green moss lined with rabbit fur, deer hair, or downy plant fibers.

Eggs: 5–8 white eggs with reddish-brown speckles concentrated around the larger end.

Tufted Titmouse
✓ Native Species
♂ / ♀ Adult (Sexes Alike)

Identification: Prominent pointed grey crest, large black eyes, black forehead patch, and peach/rust wash on flanks.

Nest: Moss, leaves, bark strips, and hair.

Eggs: 4–7 creamy white eggs with fine brown and purple speckles.

House Sparrow (Invasive Pest)
⚠️ Non-Native / Invasive (Remove)
♂ Male (Black Bib / Chestnut)
♀ Female (Dull Brown / Eyebrow)

Identification: Males have a bold black throat bib, grey crown, chestnut nape, and white cheeks. Females are plain dingy grey-brown with a distinct pale buffy stripe behind the eye.

Nest: Messy domed pile of coarse grasses, weeds, straw, trash, paper, and feathers filling the box with a side opening.

Eggs: 4–7 dull white or greenish-white eggs heavily blotched with grey and brown.

⚠️ Action Required: House Sparrows kill native bluebird adults and young in the box. In Pennsylvania, they are not protected. All House Sparrow nests and eggs must be completely removed and discarded.
